# Product

## Register

product

## Users

UniKorn serves HKUST(GZ) students who need to find campus information, compare courses and past student experiences, plan timetables, track academic progress, and participate in the campus community. They use it in short mobile sessions between classes as well as longer desktop planning sessions.

## Product Purpose

UniKorn brings course discovery, historical course reviews, timetable planning, academic progress, and community discussion into one bilingual campus tool. Success means students can find trustworthy information quickly, understand its semester context, and move between discovery and action without losing their place.

## Brand Personality

Clear, dependable, and student-centered. The product should feel like a calm campus information tool: concise enough for scanning, detailed enough for consequential course decisions, and friendly without becoming promotional.

## Anti-references

- Marketing-led landing pages that trade information density for oversized statements or decorative effects.
- Generic SaaS dashboards that make campus workflows feel like business analytics.
- Visually fragmented modules that introduce a separate component language instead of extending the established UniKorn shell.
- Interfaces that hide historical or cross-semester context behind the currently selected timetable term.

## Design Principles

1. Preserve context across navigation so students can return to the course, semester, filters, and search state they came from.
2. Separate canonical course identity from semester-specific offerings while showing both whenever the distinction matters.
3. Keep important result types discoverable instead of allowing one dense category to dominate the interface.
4. Use familiar, low-friction controls and the existing campus-blue component vocabulary across all modules.
5. Design bilingual content and mobile behavior at the same time as desktop flows.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA behavior for contrast, keyboard access, focus visibility, semantic labels, and touch targets. Respect reduced-motion preferences, avoid color-only status communication, support narrow mobile screens without horizontal overflow, and keep Chinese and English layouts equally usable.
